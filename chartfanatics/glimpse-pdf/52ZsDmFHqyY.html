Universal Criteria for All Three Strategies

Market Cap & Float Ranges

All three strategies work only on small-cap stocks with market cap between $1-100 million (first red day can go to $200M) and float between $1-50 million. Float is categorized as very low ($1-2M), mid ($2-5M), or large ($5-10M).

Minimum Stock Price: $3+

All three strategies require stocks trading above $3 per share. Stocks below this threshold mess with statistical accuracy and create execution problems.

Sectors to Avoid: Biotech, Energy, Chinese Stocks

Biotech reduces win rate by 20-30% due to unpredictable volatility. Energy sector also underperforms. Chinese stocks risk trading halts with zero liquidity to exit, causing 100%+ losses.

Pre-Market Volume Estimation

Pre-market volume is multiplied by 5-10x to estimate full-day volume. If pre-market trades 50M shares, expect 500M-1B shares for the day. Volume concentrates 9:30-11:30 AM (30-35% of daily volume).

Strategy 1: Gap Up Short

What Is a Gap Up Short

A stock gaps up 70-1000% at market open, then consolidates. The strategy shorts the breakdown after consolidation ends, targeting a 20-35% fade from the intraday high.

Gap Up Short Criteria & Filters

Market cap under 100M, float under 50M, pre-market volume under 50M (if over 50M, too crowded to trade). Avoid biotech, energy, and Chinese stocks. Gap must be above 100%.

Gap Up Short Statistics & Frequency

Gap up shorts occur 50-70 times per year with a 75%+ win rate. Average fade is 26% from intraday high. Risk-to-reward ratio is typically 1:3.5 to 1:4.

Entry & Exit for Gap Up Short

After gap up and consolidation (typically 1-2 hours), enter partial position on first breakdown. Add full position when momentum cracks 3-5% below consolidation. Stop loss above consolidation high. Target 20-35% fade.

Float-Based Sizing Rules

Position sizing depends on float category. Very low float (1-2M) allows larger positions; higher floats require smaller sizing. Average push percentage is 30-35% for 1-2M float, 20-25% for 5-10M float.

Strategy 2: Bounce Short

Bounce Short Psychology & Setup

A stock spiked to resistance on high volume 1+ year ago, then flatlined. When it gaps back up near that old resistance, trapped longs want to exit. This creates massive selling pressure that shorts ride down.

Bounce Short Dollar Block Calculation

Calculate the dollar amount stuck at the old resistance: volume traded at resistance × price. Example: 25M shares × $5 = $125M block. This must be 150M+ to be ideal. Compare to estimated intraday volume (pre-market × 10, reduced 50-80% due to selling pressure).

Bounce Short Volume Ratio & Win Rate

The ratio of trapped volume to intraday volume determines win rate. A 2:1 ratio (trapped volume 2x intraday volume) is strong; 10:1 is exceptional. Bounce shorts occur 30 times/year with 80-85% win rate.

Bounce Short Fade Patterns: Type 1 vs Type 2

Type 1: Stock gaps directly to resistance without pre-market volume (higher win rate). Type 2: Stock builds volume from bottom, goes parabolic, then fades (lower win rate). Type 1 fades 75% from top; Type 2 fades only 50%.

Bounce Short Sizing Limits

Never exceed 10% of float or 1% of daily volume. Oversizing breaks supply/demand dynamics. At large account sizes, must cover position gradually to give supply back to market and let price react naturally.

Bounce Short Entry & Exit

Enter closer to consolidation price for less risk. Exit gradually on the way down, not all at once, to allow supply back into market. The closer entry to consolidation, the more size you can take.

Strategy 3: First Red Day

First Red Day Setup Requirements

Stock must have 3+ consecutive green days with increasing volume and 300%+ range (or 1000%+ for 2-day setup). No red days allowed in between; one red day resets the count. Market cap under 200M ideal.

First Red Day: Predicting the Top

Stocks stop rising when they hit a dollar amount ceiling based on initial market cap. Under $50M cap: ~$1B; $100M cap: ~$3B; $200M+ cap: $5-10B. Calculate daily volatility (30-50%) to predict which day reaches the ceiling.

First Red Day Statistics & Frequency

First red day occurs 5-10 times per year with 90%+ win rate. Unlike gap up and bounce shorts (max 1-1.2M size), first red day allows multi-million share positions, enabling account growth from single trades.

First Red Day Entry Strategy

On the final green day (day before first red), enter only 1/4 position at open because momentum is still strong and bounces rarely occur. Wait for second day (first red day) when pre-market volume drops 50-80% to enter remaining 3/4 position. Stop loss above consolidation.

First Red Day Risk Management

Average risk on first red day is 10-15% (stop above consolidation), but win rate is 90%+, making risk-to-reward favorable. Avoid shorting on the final green day when volume is extreme; wait for second day when volume drops significantly.

First Red Day vs Gap/Bounce Shorts

Gap and bounce shorts max out at 1-1.2M shares per trade; first red day allows 10M+ shares. This is why first red day is the primary wealth-building strategy. The larger size combined with 90%+ win rate enables exponential account growth.

Key Insights & Takeaways

Statistics Drive Sizing & Conviction

Win rate and volume ratios determine position size. Higher win rates and volume ratios = larger positions. Lower metrics = smaller positions. This data-driven approach prevents overleveraging on low-probability setups.

Human Psychology + Market Mechanics = Edge

All three strategies combine human psychology (trapped longs, FOMO, panic selling) with market mechanics (supply/demand, volume concentration, dollar blocks). Strategies without both elements fail.

Larger Account Sizes Require Different Game

At multi-million dollar position sizes, Steven must cover gradually and manage market impact. Oversizing breaks supply/demand and turns winning setups into losses. Precision becomes even more critical.

10 Years of Hand-Tracked Data

Steven tracked every trade by hand from 2015-2025, recording market cap, float, volume, sectors, and outcomes. This manual process revealed the precise criteria that work. No shortcuts; data is the foundation.

Avoid Crowded Tickers on First Green Day

If pre-market volume exceeds 50M or float rotates 15+ times, reduce size significantly or skip. Crowded tickers have reduced fade potential and higher risk of breakouts. Wait for less crowded setups.
