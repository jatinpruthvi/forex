Long Setup: The Bullish Cycle

Price Cycle Foundation

The long strategy is built on Oliver Kell's price cycle concept, viewing uptrends through the lens of the wedge pop, EMA cross-back, base formation, and breakout—a continuation setup within an established uptrend.

Entry Method 1: Buying on Strength

Buy when price breaks above the horizontal consolidation level (the breakout level) with confirmation. Set stop loss at the low of the current or prior day, creating a defined risk entry with multiples of potential reward.

Entry Method 2: Buying on Weakness

In choppy or extended markets, buy pullbacks into the 10 and 20-day EMAs rather than waiting for breakout confirmation. This provides much tighter risk-reward than buying on strength, especially when breakouts tend to fade.

Stock Selection Criteria

Trade stocks with at least $50 million average daily dollar volume, more than 3% average daily range (ADR), strong group momentum, and relative strength versus the market during consolidation periods.

Trade Management: Scaling Out

Take 1/3 of position off when profit reaches 2x the average daily range. Take another 1/3 when price extends 8-10x ATR from the 50-day moving average. Trail the final 1/3 using the 10 or 20-day EMA as a stop, exiting on violation.

Timeframe Approach

Daily chart is used for setup identification and context, while 5-minute chart is used for precise entry timing and stop placement, allowing traders to catch intraday momentum within the larger daily setup.

Short Setup: The Bearish Cycle

Failed Base Pattern

Short setups target stocks that have been in an uptrend, formed a base, attempted to break out but failed—this is a late-stage failed base pattern that signals a shift from bullish to bearish structure.

Invalidation Signal: 20-Day EMA Violation

The first critical signal is when a stock violates its 20-day exponential moving average on larger volume than recent bars, indicating a shift in character and triggering placement on the breakdown watchlist.

Wedging Action and Entry

After violating the 20-day EMA, watch for the stock to attempt a recovery but form a wedge pattern on minimal volume—this shows weak buying demand. Short the turn lower with stop just above the high of day.

Market Confirmation

Ideal short entries occur when the broader market (QQQ) is also declining at the same time, providing 'wind at your back' and confirming the downtrend context.

Cover Strategy: Undercut and Rally

Cover half the position when price undercuts prior lows and then reclaims them (undercut and rally area). Cover the remaining half when price reclaims the 10-day EMA. Look for re-entry as price trades back into the 20-day EMA.

Aggressive Profit Taking on Shorts

Unlike longs where positions are trailed for extended moves, shorts are managed more actively. Take larger portions of profit earlier because downside moves come quickly and reversals can erase gains rapidly.

Simplicity Over Complexity

Analysis Paralysis from Over-Complication

Early in his trading career, Clement used complex chart layouts with multiple indicators (MACD, RSI, stochastics, money flow index) that generated conflicting signals and prevented him from executing trades. Removing complexity solved this problem.

Win Rate vs Risk-Reward Focus

The strategy has a 30% historical win rate, but this is acceptable because the focus is on the risk-reward game—when you win, you win big; when you lose, losses are small and predefined. This asymmetry generates positive expectancy.

Objective Entry and Exit Rules

Simple setups keep trading objective: you know exactly where your entry is, where your stop is, and when the trade is invalidated. This removes emotion and allows consistent execution.

Minimal Setup Requirements

Clement trades from a single 27-inch monitor with just price, moving averages, volume, and MACD—no complex multi-screen setup. This simplicity forces focus on what matters most.

Execution and Psychology

Training Your Eyes Through Historical Study

Identify the biggest winners year-by-year on daily charts, study their setups, understand the market context when entries occurred, and use AI tools to research why these stocks moved. This builds pattern recognition for real-time identification.

Market Context is Critical

Understanding where the market is in its cycle (correction, recovery, extended) informs which setups to prioritize. During corrections, look for stocks showing relative strength; during recoveries, leaders emerge from the base-building phase.

Re-entry Discipline and Revenge Trading Prevention

Short sellers may get wicked out of tight stops and need to re-enter. However, limit re-entries to a maximum of 3 attempts per ticker to prevent revenge trading and emotional decision-making.

Reaction vs Anticipation Balance

Trading is 70% reaction (flowing with what the market shows) and 30% anticipation (studying cycles and preparing for what's next). Come into the day with an opinion but be ready to abandon it if the market refutes it.

Advanced Concepts

Relative Strength as a Leading Indicator

Stocks showing relative strength during market corrections (trading sideways or up while the market declines) are likely to lead the recovery. This is a key filter for identifying which setups will generate the biggest moves.

Volume as Supply-Demand Confirmation

Receding volume during consolidation signals low supply and high probability of a breakout. Large volume on the wedge pop confirms demand. Minimal volume on recovery attempts after a breakdown confirms weak buying.

Base Duration and Move Magnitude

Larger bases (6+ weeks) tend to generate larger moves than small bases. The saying 'the bigger the base, the higher the space' reflects the accumulation principle—longer consolidation periods indicate more conviction building.

ATR-Based Position Sizing and Scaling

Use average true range (ATR) to define position size and scaling rules: take 1/3 off at 2x ADR profit, another 1/3 at 8-10x ATR extension from the 50-day, and trail the final 1/3. This scales profit-taking to volatility.

Five-Minute Chart for Precision Entries

While the daily chart identifies the setup, the 5-minute chart provides precise entry timing. Look for breakouts of the opening range, VWAP reclaims, or moving average tests on the intraday timeframe to time entries within the larger daily setup.
