Core Strategy Framework

8020 Strategy Overview

The 8020 strategy is a NASDAQ-focused mean-reversion system named after the last two digits of price levels. For example, at 25,680 the trader watches the 80 level; at 25,620 the 20 level. These levels are where price consistently reacts, making them primary entry and exit zones.

Two Timeframe Setup

Okala uses a 10-minute chart for overall market structure and a 200-second chart (one-third of 10 minutes) for precise entries. The 200-second breaks down the 10-minute candle into three equal parts, allowing granular entry identification while maintaining structural context.

Risk Management: 10-Point Stop

Every trade uses a fixed 10-point stop-loss. If price moves 10 points against the entry, the trader exits. This discipline reflects the principle that being wrong by 10 points usually means being wrong by 50-100 points, so early exit prevents larger losses.

Multi-Contract Position Sizing

Okala trades multiple contracts per entry rather than single contracts. At the first 15-point profit target, he covers initial risk by taking 1-2 contracts off. Remaining contracts are trailed to break-even, then allowed to run for larger moves. Risk per trade stays static in points; position size increases when the trader feels 'hot.'

Trading Hours and Volatility Windows

Okala primarily trades the New York open (9:30 AM ET) when volatility and variance are highest, giving his statistical edge the best conditions. He avoids the lunch hour (11 AM–1 PM) when the market is choppy and lacks directional bias, and stays out of slow trending or choppy conditions where the edge breaks down.

Three Entry Structures

Fork Setup (Mean Reversion Long)

A fork forms after a strong downmove when price puts in a long-wick, small-body candle (the 'initiation candle'). The next candle tests the low but doesn't break it, then makes a higher high. Entry is on this higher-high candle, targeting the previous low. This catches late shorts being squeezed out.

H-Pattern Setup (Continuation Short)

An H-pattern forms when price bounces off a level (e.g., 20), misses a reversal entry, then makes a strong move into the opposite level (e.g., 80) with a long wick. Price then rolls over and builds a lowercase-H shape. Entry is on the rollover or cross-section test, targeting continuation lower.

Cross-Section Entry (Structural Confluence)

A cross-section is where two breakdown candles intersect. When price retests this exact intersection point (especially at a level like 20 or 80), it provides a high-confluence entry. Cross-sections work on any timeframe and can be used for both longs and shorts depending on market structure.

Repair Candle & Magnet Concept

Repair Candle Definition

A repair candle is one that opens and moves in one direction (e.g., down) with no wick on the opposite side (e.g., no top wick). This means no participants entered at higher prices; all their limit orders remain unfilled above or below the candle, creating a 'magnet' for price to return to.

Using Repair Candles as Targets

Repair candles serve as take-profit magnets. If a fork or H-pattern entry is taken, the repair candle from a prior move becomes a target where price is likely to revert. This allows traders to size into entries more aggressively (taking more than 15 points on TP1) because the magnet provides high-confidence exit.

Trading Discipline & Common Pitfalls

Overtrading & Entry Discipline

The 8020 strategy generates many entries daily, tempting traders to take subpar setups. Okala warns that taking entries that miss one criterion (e.g., no level tap, no fork low test) leads to losses. The best entries combine all elements: level, structure, and candle pattern. Overtrading is the most common failure mode.

No Fixed Trade Limit; Condition-Based

Rather than a hard rule like 'three trades per day max,' Okala uses market conditions. If the market is 'hot' and providing clear setups with all criteria met, he steps on the gas and takes more trades. If choppy or slow, he reduces activity. This requires honest self-assessment of whether entries are genuine or forced.

Consistency Rules in Prop Firms

Prop firms often enforce consistency rules (e.g., daily profit must stay within a range). A $3,000 day when average is $300–600 can violate this and jeopardize the payout. Okala emphasizes knowing your firm's rules and managing position size accordingly, especially when letting runners go.
