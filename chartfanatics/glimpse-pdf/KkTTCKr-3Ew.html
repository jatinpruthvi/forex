The Three-Step System Overview

Why the System Was Built

Abraham created this systematic approach because he was wasting time jumping between timeframes without a clear process to identify opportunities. The system cuts that wasted time by providing a step-by-step framework that works repeatedly.

Step 1: Identify Market Context (Macro Timeframes)

Use 4-hour and daily charts to identify the overall trend and where price is in the liquidity cycle. Markets move in trends with higher highs/lows, then pull back to fair value (internal range liquidity) before continuing. This context tells you whether a pullback is likely or if price will push higher.

Step 2: Confirm Bias with Secondary Structure (15m/1h)

On 15-minute and 1-hour charts, look for the secondary structure (the pullbacks and impulses within the macro move). This structure must align with your macro bias. For example, if macro says pullback is coming, you need to see weakness in the secondary structure (volume, closure below previous highs) before shorting.

Step 3: Execute with Entry Model + Orderflow Confirmation (Micro Timeframes)

Once macro and secondary align, use one of three entry models on 1m/5m/30s charts (depending on asset volatility) and confirm with bookmap orderflow. The entry model is just the trigger; orderflow shows whether aggressive buyers or sellers are actually entering, validating the setup.

The Three Entry Models

Entry Model 1: Inverted Fair Value Gap (IFBG)

Price takes liquidity above a previous high, creates a fair value gap, then closes with volume below that gap. This invalidates the bullish move and signals a reversal. Requires orderflow confirmation showing sellers gaining control; without it, the setup fails frequently.

Entry Model 2: Change of Character (Preferred)

In an uptrend, price reacts at support, makes a candle suggesting a sell, then manipulates with volume, breaks, retests, and closes below with volume—signaling a reversal. This is a complete cycle and works in both directions (flipped for downtrends). Requires liquidity to be swept first for best performance.

Entry Model 3: Break and Retest (Continuation)

Price breaks a level with volume and retests it, then continues in the original direction. Must see a fair value gap displacing the previous high and retest closing above the apex, aligned with orderflow showing aggressive buyers.

Critical Nuance: Entry Model ≠ Confirmation

The entry model is only a trigger, not confirmation. Confirmation comes from your full macro and secondary analysis plus orderflow volume. Many traders mark every IFBG and fail because they ignore this distinction. Only take the setup when everything aligns.

Orderflow & Bookmap Fundamentals

What Bookmap Shows: Three Layers

Bookmap displays the real-time order book in three parts: (1) heatmap = passive orders sitting at price levels waiting to be hit; (2) volume dots = aggressive market orders entering right now (these move price); (3) current order book = contracts at each price level. The heatmap attracts price like a magnet but doesn't move it; aggressors do.

Point of Control (POC) and VWAP

POC is the price level with the most volume in the session profile; it acts as support/resistance and recalculates as new volume enters. VWAP is anchored to the open and more dynamic. Both are powerful because they show where institutions are concentrated and when they lose control (POC flips, VWAP breaks).

Reading Orderflow: Buyers vs. Sellers

When you see large volume spikes on the buy side (green), buyers are aggressive. When you see large spikes on the sell side (red), sellers are aggressive. If POC is moving down, bearish volume is dominating. If buyers can't push price above a level despite high buy volume, sellers are absorbing those buys—a bearish signal.

Spoofing and Fake Orders

Large orders that disappear before price reaches them are likely spoofs—fake orders placed to manipulate price action. Algorithms use these to induce traders into positions. Recognize them by watching for orders that vanish without execution.

Key Principles & Mindset

No Mechanical Strategies Work Long-Term

Abraham doesn't believe in pure mechanical strategies because if one worked all the time, everyone would use it and it would stop working. Instead, he uses entry models as triggers for discretionary analysis. The system is repeatable but requires judgment about market context.

Confluence Over Single Signals

High-probability setups have multiple confluences: macro bias aligned, secondary structure confirmed, entry model triggered, and orderflow showing aggression. The more confluences, the higher the win rate. Back-test and journal to identify which confluences matter most for your edge.

Patience is the Most Powerful Tool

Abraham repeatedly emphasizes waiting for price to come to you rather than forcing trades. If a setup doesn't have all confirmations, skip it. If market conditions are sketchy, close early or stay out. Patience prevents losses and lets high-probability setups play out.

ICT + Orderflow Ends the Debate

Abraham uses both ICT concepts (market structure, liquidity, fair value gaps) and orderflow (bookmap aggression). They're not competing—they're complementary. ICT teaches you where price should go; orderflow shows you when institutions are actually making it go there.
