This consistency compounds into six-figure monthly payouts and scales accounts reliably. The key is sizing down during high volatility and letting the points do the heavy lifting.

The Multi-Timeframe Reversal Model

Daily Sweep → 4-Hour Inversion → 15-Minute Confirmation

The core setup stacks three timeframe layers: (1) Market sweeps monthly/weekly highs or lows (liquidity grab), (2) Creates a 4-hour fair value gap on the reaction, (3) Inverts that 4-hour gap (bias confirmation), (4) On the 15-minute, a bullish fair value gap forms during the retracement, (5) That 15-minute gap gets inverted on the 1- or 5-minute (entry trigger). Each layer must respect the previous one.

Fair Value Gap Inversion as Primary Confirmation

An inversion occurs when price breaks through a fair value gap (bullish or bearish) and then rejects it. For Desi, this is the highest-probability confirmation signal because it shows the market has changed intent. He pairs this with order blocks and previous resistance/support for extra confluence.

Prioritize Monthly/Weekly Liquidity Over Session Highs

When identifying draw liquidity (areas where traders are positioned), monthly and weekly highs/lows carry higher reversal probability than daily or session-level liquidity. Equal highs and lows stacked at these levels are especially high-probability reversal zones.

Day Trading Execution: Precision and Sizing

Entry: Market Execute on Lower Timeframe Inversion

Once the higher timeframe bias is confirmed (daily sweep + 4-hour inversion), Desi market-executes on the 1- or 5-minute inversion rather than using limit orders. This ensures he captures the move before it runs away, especially in high-volatility sessions.

Stop Loss Above 15-Minute High; Target at Prior Lows

The stop loss sits above the current 15-minute high to allow the trade to breathe and avoid whipsaws. The target is the prior sellside liquidity (often the 9:30 a.m. low on New York open or previous session lows). This typically yields a 1.5:1 to 2:1 initial risk-reward.

Trim 50% at First Target, Move Stop to Breakeven, Let Runners Go

Desi always trims half his position at the first take-profit level, immediately moves the remaining stop loss to breakeven, and lets the runners capture additional liquidity (trend-line lows, unfilled daily gaps, etc.). This locks in a win while maintaining upside exposure.

Size Down in High Volatility; Let Points Do the Work

When the market is moving 200–400 points per day, Desi reduces contract size so that the same dollar risk applies. This prevents account blowups during volatile sessions while allowing him to capture larger point moves (e.g., 50 points of draw-down becomes manageable).

Two-Loss Rule: Stop Trading for the Day

If Desi takes two consecutive losses, he stops trading for the day. If he wins one and loses one, he allows himself a third attempt. This rule protects capital and prevents revenge trading during low-probability windows.

Swing Trading: Higher Timeframe Patience

Entry Off Hourly or 4-Hour, Never Below 1-Hour

For swing trades, Desi enters only off the hourly or 4-hour timeframe, never the 15-minute or lower. This ensures the trade is aligned with genuine higher-timeframe moves rather than intraday noise. The wider stop loss and longer hold time justify this discipline.

Mark Multiple Partials: Daily Gaps, Weekly Gaps, Hourly Gaps

Rather than one take-profit, swing trades have multiple partial targets based on unfilled daily, weekly, and hourly fair value gaps. This allows trimming into strength while maintaining exposure to larger moves.

Ignore Intraday Whipsaws; Focus on Multi-Day Context

A swing trade that retraces 40–50 points intraday is noise if the higher-timeframe bias remains intact. Desi ignores these moves and only manages the position if the hourly or 4-hour structure breaks, not on 15-minute or 5-minute chop.

Options Leaps for Long-Term Positions

For swing trades, Desi uses 1–2 month out options contracts (leaps) to reduce daily management stress. This allows him to hold positions through minor retracements without worrying about time decay or intraday volatility.

Long-Term Leap Trading: Monthly/Weekly Context

Monthly Fair Value Gaps and Order Blocks as Macro Targets

Accumulate on Monthly Inversion Confirmation

Once a monthly fair value gap inverts (price breaks through and rejects it), Desi begins adding long-term positions. This inversion signals a potential reversal of the macro trend and justifies deploying larger capital into leap options or long-term futures.

Apply Day-Trading Model to Daily Charts Within Macro Bias

Once the monthly/weekly bias is bullish (e.g., post-inversion), every daily chart setup becomes a high-probability day-trade opportunity. The macro context provides conviction, and the daily model provides precise entries and exits.

Volatility Spikes Create Generational Opportunities

Building Your Own Model: Key Struggles and Solutions

Initial Paralysis: Too Many Concepts, Not Enough Clarity

When Desi first learned ICT, he tried to use every tool at once (gaps, order blocks, liquidity, SMT, etc.), leading to analysis paralysis. He solved this by isolating the two most important elements for his trading: liquidity and confirmation (inversion).

Timeframe Filtering: Stick to Consistent Levels

Rather than jumping between 1-minute, 5-minute, 15-minute, and hourly charts, Desi recommends staying consistent. For swing trades, use daily/4-hour/hourly. For day trades, use 15-minute/5-minute/1-minute. Avoid going too low (5-second charts) or forcing setups across too many timeframes.

Live Testing Over Backtesting

Desi built his model through live testing on prop firm accounts (lower capital risk) rather than pure backtesting. This added psychological pressure and forced him to refine his rules based on real execution, not just historical data.

Confidence Comes From Data, Not Perfection

Desi's win rate is approximately 65%, meaning he loses regularly. But because he has 1,000–2,000 trades of data showing the model works, he trusts it through losing streaks. New traders lack this data and quit too early.

ICT Is Fractal: Adapt to Your Personality

ICT concepts (gaps, inversions, liquidity, order blocks) can be applied at any timeframe and combined in different ways. Ten ICT traders in a room will trade the same setup slightly differently. The key is building a model that resonates with your personality and testing it until you have conviction.
